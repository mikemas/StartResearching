Last edited 10:14 pm PST. </strong></span> </p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">In addition to the <a href="/voynich-blog/visual-elements-of-plato-within-the-voynich">“Visual Elements of Plato within the Voynich”</a> I’ve discussed in the previous blog post, in which I link parts the Astrological section of the Voynich to <em>Timaeus, part 1</em> of Plato, and parts of the Biological section to the <em>Timaeus, part 2, </em>I am noticing many more Pythagorean aspects throughout the whole text.  Like the monad circle within the circle diagram on Folio 57v.  And perhaps the Botany section has to do with the vegetarianism of Pythagoreans? </p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>Anyways, I believe the starred paragraph section of the Voynich are a collection of sayings, or moral maxims (or ethical aphorisms, or gnomes), of Pythagorean beliefs, of the specific collections proposed below.</strong>  In fact, I propose we rename this section of the Voynich <strong>“The Starred Sentences”</strong> and no longer refer to them as recipes. </p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Originally I thought it was the “Sentences of Sextus”, but this seems like too high an amount of 451 (or larger) and so I looked at which collections these were drawn from, and came much closer.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">In this section of the Voynich we have <strong>324 stars</strong>, each aligned more or less with a sentence or a few. There are also at least two missing folios (109r / 109v and 110r / 110v). Each folio averages around 14 stars, with some like 103r higher at 19, and three folios at the lowest end of 10 stars (105r, 105v, and 116r), and the rest vary in amount. So if we assume our 4 missing folios at 14 stars each, it would give us 56 added stars, or <strong>380 total</strong> goal (324 we have plus 56 estimated missing = 380).  With me so far? </p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Here’s what I suggest: </p><ul data-rte-list="default"><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">The Pythagorean Sentences = 123</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">The Sentences of Clitarchus = 130* </p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">The Golden Sentences of Democrates = 80 (some have 84?)</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">The Similitudes of Demophilus = 59** </p></li></ul><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Adding these up we get 392.  Pretty close to our 380 estimate. </p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>Update (as of Sunday night, May 24th): I have a possible new order, without the Sentences of Clitarchus, but adding in the Sentences of Demophilis, and the Golden Versus:</strong></p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>Pythag. Sentences - 123<br>Sentences of Democrates - 80<br>Similitudes of Demophilis - 59<br>Sentences of Demophilis - 45<br>Golden Versus - 71<br>Total of this combination = 378<br><br>This version is closer to my estimate of 380 than the other combo with 392.</strong>&nbsp;&nbsp;</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">It would mean our missing four folios would have to total 68, or average of 17 stars per page. Well within existing Folios (remember 103r has 19).  I wonder if Folio 58r / 58v belongs in this section as well, which would bring our missing stars total down.   </p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">I would love it if some experts could confirm these numbers, I’m just going off the sources I have found. *Per Chadwick, the manuscripts and maxims in the Sentences of Clitarchus are: </p><ul data-rte-list="default"><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">1) Parisinus gr. 1630, fol. 186 = 93 maxims</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">2) Vaticanus gr. 1144, saec. xv, fol. 232  = 59 - 22 contained above = 37</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"> 3) Bodleianus Auct. F. 6. 26, saec. xv, foil. 183–7 = 38, all within 93 of Par.</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">93 + 37 = 130.</p></li></ul><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">**As for The Similitudes of Demophilus, there are 45 in Sallustius, but 59 in Bridgman - and I also counted 59 in <a href="https://archive.org/details/demophilidemocr00holsgoog/page/n22/mode/1up" target="_blank">a 1638 text</a>. </p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">So our variables are 1) how many are sayings are actually in each collection, and 2) how many stars are we actually missing in our four missing folios. </p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Let me give some selections of the above listed collections:</p><ul data-rte-list="default"><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>The Pythagorean Sentences </strong>(From my understanding, the term “Pythagorean Sentences” can mean a wide encompassing selection of many of the fragments named in this post, or it can be a specific collection of 123 maxims which include some of the fragments - is this right?) <a href="https://archive.org/details/sentencesofsextu0000henr/page/84/mode/1up" target="_blank">(*Greek version from Chadwick’s Sentences of Sextus)</a></p><ul data-rte-list="default"><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Edit: Another point I want to make!
