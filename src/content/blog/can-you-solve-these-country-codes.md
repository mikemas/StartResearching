<div class="sqs-html-content" data-sqsp-text-block-content>
  <p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">This is a bit different of a post today - one in which I am asking for your help to solve. I have posed this question to several different genealogists, asked around in Facebook groups, and have done diligent research and have yet to come up with an answer, so I’m posing this to all of you. </p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">We’ve talked a lot about U.S. passenger arrival lists lately. I’ve shown in my last blog post how <a href="https://www.startresearching.com/blog/passenger-lists-pt-2-the-meaning" target="_blank">the Immigration Act of 1924 codes</a> show up as handwritten notations near the visa columns relating to the type of immigrant, such as “3 - 2” for a government official, or “4 - B” for a returning immigrant that was legally admitted before. I’ve also reviewed<a href="/blog/passenger-lists-the-meaning-behind-the-notations-abbreviations-and-marks" target="_blank"> additional abbreviations and marks </a>in an earlier post. </p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">However, in my research and review of all of this, I’ve noticed additional notations in many passenger lists for those arriving to the U.S. in the early 20th century, that seem to be related to the locations. I’m calling these handwritten notations “country codes” for now, but keep in mind they could be related to visas, immigration quotas, taxes, passenger counts, race of people, or something else entirely.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">My question is - what does these numbers mean, and is there reference material for them we can cross-reference?  And of course, in understanding the meaning, do they offer any more information for general genealogy research?</p>

<div class="sqs-html-content" data-sqsp-text-block-content>
  <p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Here’s a breakdown of what I’ve noticed about these codes: </p><ul data-rte-list="default"><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">The numbers in the “Place of Birth” column are often four digits in XX - XX format, but they can also appear as fractions</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">The numbers in the “Last Permanent Residence” column are often two digits, XX</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">In the “Place of Birth” column, the country could have more than one number. Note in the image above England has both an “03” and “04”. </p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">In the “Last Permanent Residence” column on the right, these seem more static and more likely country codes unique to each location. </p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">There are some similarities between different passenger lists using the same codes (such as England being “04 - 15” in the first and second example), but more differences than similarities. Japan and the U.S. are usually “99” but other countries, such as Chili and Panama in the image below, have also been “99”. Canada is usually 80. </p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">When there is a column for “Race or People” there are also two digit numbers in that column, but only two digits in “Place of Birth” as opposed to the other pattern of XX - XX. Perhaps this means the first part of the four digit number in the “Place of Birth” in the other passenger lists has to do with race? </p></li></ul>
