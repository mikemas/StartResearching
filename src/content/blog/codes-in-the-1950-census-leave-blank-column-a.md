By Sherri Mastrangelo.</span></p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Update: When writing this blog post initially, I formed the chart through comparison of over 50 enumeration districts (the first to show for every state on the National Archives search results). <div class="sqs-html-content" data-sqsp-text-block-content>
  <p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">In the 1950 Census, between columns 8 for Relationship and 9 for Race, is “Leave Blank A”.  This column is hand-written later, after the census taker has visited homes, and refers to the individual’s relationship to the head of household. You’ll notice that column 8, immediately to the left, also describes the relationship. So are these numbers actually telling us anything new? </p>

<div class="sqs-html-content" data-sqsp-text-block-content>
  <p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Well, yes and no - but before we get into the usefulness of these notes let me share my findings.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">I was getting asked this questions in a few emails, and noticed it in a few Facebook genealogy groups as well: “What do the numbers in the first Leave Blank” column mean?”. Some other members in one of the Facebook groups dismissed the notes as not important and not worth looking into, while others speculated it had to do with a widow status. </p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">I came across this section on Stevemorse.org called “Deciphering Miscellaneous Codes Appended to the 1950 Census in One Step” by Stephen P. Morse, PhD and Joel D. Weintraub, PhD: <a href="https://stevemorse.org/census/mcodes1950.htm"><span style="text-decoration:underline">https://stevemorse.org/census/mcodes1950.htm</span></a><span style="text-decoration:underline"> </span>that was compiled before the release of the census, which seemed to have the answers in the first dropdown for Column A. Yet I quickly noticed that these answers, that included “0” for head of household, “1” for wife, “2” for child, and so on, did not match what I was seeing on actual 1950 census records (they align more with the 1940 census).  <strong><em>Update 16 April: this site has now been updated!</em></strong></p>
