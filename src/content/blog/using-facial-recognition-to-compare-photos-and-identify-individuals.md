  <h3 style="white-space:pre-wrap;">December 20, 2020</h3><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">By now, you’re probably aware of the facial recognition built in to many photo storage programs. When you upload a new photo, a program like Google will automatically try to identify who is in the photo, based on labels you have previously created from other photos you have stored.  Every now and then you will have to correct a label or two, but I find it to be highly accurate. </p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">It may not have occurred to you that you can use these programs to identify individuals in your stack of old ancestor photos - if you have at least one other photo to compare with, that is confirmed to be the individual in question.  </p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">How many of us have old photos we know must be ONE of our ancestors, but we can’t figure out who? I see many posts across various Facebook genealogy groups, asking if two different pictures could be the same person, so I know I’m not alone in this!</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Programs that currently have facial recognition for public use: </p><ul data-rte-list="default"><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><a href="https://photos.google.com/" target="_blank">Google Photos</a> - free cloud storage</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><a href="https://www.apple.com/ios/photos/">Apple Photos App</a> - free on Apple devices (Mac, iPad, iPhone)</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><a href="https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/help/face-recognition.html" target="_blank">Adobe Photoshop Lightroom Classic</a> - desktop app for purchase </p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><a href="https://aws.amazon.com/getting-started/hands-on/detect-analyze-compare-faces-rekognition/" target="_blank">Amazon Rekognition</a> - developer API with free 12 month trial as part of the AWS Free Tier</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><a href="https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/services/cognitive-services/face/" target="_blank">Face API </a>- Microsoft Azure developer tool, free 12 month trial with an Azure account</p></li></ul><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">After uploading to <strong>Google Photos</strong>, you may have to wait a bit for the facial recognition to kick in (up to 24 hours) and be sure that the feature for “face grouping” is turned on in your settings. You will see the results grouped in a “People” album, without names unless they match someone already given a name in your photos. The accuracy is pretty good, but not 100%. </p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">There are also online reverse image searches, in which you upload a photo or a link to a photo, and search for similar images online. Some, like <a href="https://images.google.com/">Google Images</a>, are not based on facial recognition but on similarities within whole images. Others, like <a href="https://pimeyes.com/en" target="_blank">PimEyes</a>, actually use facial recognition technology - but it might cost you. </p>
